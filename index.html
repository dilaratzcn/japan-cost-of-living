CONTEXT

Why does the CPI matter?

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures changes over time in the prices of goods and services acquired by households. Separating headline CPI from core series, physical goods, and services helps distinguish temporary price swings from broader shifts in the cost of living.

Year-over-Year (YoY) Price Change (%)

Calculated from monthly seasonally adjusted CPI series as ((CPI_t − CPI_{t−12}) / CPI_{t−12}) × 100. Values above 0% indicate annual price increases; values below 0% indicate declines.

EVERYDAY IMPACT

Why compare prices and wages together?

Price movements alone do not capture whether everyday life feels more affordable. Comparing wages with consumer prices shows how much buying power monthly earnings retain over time.

04 / PURCHASING POWER COMPARISON

Prices vs. Wages: Relative Growth & Purchasing Power

Both series are normalized to Jan 2010 = 100 on the primary axis, alongside the Purchasing Power Index ((Wage Index / CPI All Items) × 100) on the secondary axis.

"If wages increase more slowly than prices, purchasing power decreases."

Methodology & Limitations: Both series share a 2020 = 100 base and monthly frequency across 201 overlapping months from Jan 2010 to Sep 2026. Re-indexing both to Jan 2010 = 100 highlights cumulative divergence since 2010. The wage dataset reflects contractual cash earnings (regular pay excluding bonuses) at establishments with 30+ employees.

07 / DOCUMENTATION & METHODOLOGY

About the Data

Provenance, variables, units, and methodological limitations of both datasets.

Dataset 1: Consumer Price Index (zmi2020c.csv)

  • Source: Consumer Price Index series (zmi2020c); agency name not embedded in CSV header rows—verify Statistics Bureau of Japan / e-Stat citation.
  • What It Measures: Seasonally adjusted monthly price indices for 5 categories: All items (0901), All items less fresh food (0902), Less fresh food & energy (0906), Goods (0921), and Services (0924).
  • Time Period & Units: Jan 2010 – Oct 2026 (202 monthly rows, no missing observations). Base: 2020 average = 100.
  • Limitations: Seasonally adjusted values smooth regular seasonal patterns; basket weight cells are blank in this CSV extract.

Dataset 2: Monthly Labour Survey (hon-t14.xls)

  • Source: Monthly Labour Survey (毎月勤労統計調査, hon-t14.xls; Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare).
  • What It Measures: Wage indices and official YoY percentage changes for Contractual Cash Earnings (きまって支給する給与) at establishments with 30+ employees (total employment types) across Total Industries (TL) and 16 sectors (C–R).
  • Time Period & Units: Monthly up to Sep 2026 (series start dates vary by sector: 1952, 1970, 2000, or 2010). Base: 2020 average = 100; YoY in %.
  • Limitations: Excludes irregular special cash earnings (bonuses) and establishments with fewer than 30 employees; earlier pre-inception cells are marked "-".
Educational Disclaimer: This interactive dashboard was developed for a university Data Visualization project. Aggregate indices reflect statistical averages across surveyed households and establishments (30+ employees) and do not represent the exact living expenses or wage trajectory of every individual.
